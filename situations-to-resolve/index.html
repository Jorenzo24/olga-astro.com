The one-question reading

What happens when you bring a single question

Horary is one of the oldest branches of astrology, and one of the most practical. Instead of mapping your whole life, it answers a single, specific question by casting a chart for the exact moment that question is understood and asked. That moment becomes the chart, and the chart holds the answer. It is astrology turned toward one clear matter, here and now.

What makes this reading unusual is that it asks nothing of your birth data. You do not need to know your time of birth, your rising sign or your whole chart. You need only a sincere question that genuinely matters to you, and the moment you bring it. That is why so many people turn to horary when something is pressing and they simply want a focused answer rather than a full life reading.

It is important to be honest about what this reading offers. Horary gives you a clear reading of the situation and grounded guidance to act on, not a guarantee of the future. It will show you the real shape of the matter, who and what is involved, and the most likely direction of things, so you can decide with more clarity and less guesswork. It works best with one clear question at a time.

What you can ask

Six kinds of question this reading is made for

One chart, read around one question, because this reading is at its sharpest when it stays focused.

Should I take this step?

A decision you keep circling, a job, a move, an offer, read clearly so you can choose with more confidence.

Where is the lost thing?

A missing object, a misplaced document, even a question of whether it will turn up at all, one of horary's oldest uses.

Is now the right time?

When timing is everything, the chart reads whether the moment favours acting now or waiting a little longer.

Will this work out?

A sale, a deal, an application or a plan: the likely direction of an outcome you are waiting on.

What is really going on?

A confusing situation between people, where you sense more than you can see, named honestly.

Should I trust this?

A choice that asks you to commit, in love, money or work, weighed clearly when your own instinct feels divided.
